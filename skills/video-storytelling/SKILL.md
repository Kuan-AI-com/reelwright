# Video storytelling for explainers

A video is a promise and its payoff. Plan the **question** the viewer will want answered, delay the answer just long enough, then pay it off clearly.

## 1. Find the spine (before writing any scene)

Write these three lines first. If you can't, the topic isn't ready.
- **Question:** what the viewer will wonder in the first 3 s. ("Why are there two high tides a day, not one?")
- **Turn:** the non-obvious idea that answers it. ("There are two bulges: one pulled toward the Moon, one left behind.")
- **Takeaway:** the one sentence they could repeat to a friend.

## 2. Beat templates

| Length | Beats (≈ scenes) |
|---|---|
| 15–30 s (Shorts/Reels) | Hook with the surprising fact → the turn in one visual → takeaway. 3–4 scenes. |
| 45–60 s | Hook → context (why it matters, 1 scene) → mechanism (2–3 scenes) → turn → takeaway. 5–7 scenes. |
| 90–180 s | Hook → stakes → mechanism in steps → a common misconception and why it's wrong → evidence (data) → takeaway. 8–14 scenes. |

Rules: something new every 5–8 seconds; the first scene states the question; the turn arrives before 70% of the runtime; the last scene answers the first.

## 3. Pick the right visual (Reelwright scene types)

| The information is… | Use | Not |
|---|---|---|
| A single striking number | `stat` (with `source`) | bullets with the number buried in text |
| 2–6 quantities to compare | `bars` (same unit, with `source`) | several `stat` scenes in a row |
| Two options, before/after, myth/fact | `compare` | bullets "pros/cons" |
| A process or 2–5 parallel points | `bullets` (≤ 9 words each) | a paragraph on screen |
| A real, attributable line from a person | `quote` (with `author`) | invented "as experts say" |
| The question / the takeaway | `title` / `outro` | |

Vary types: never three of the same in a row; bullets in at most a third of the scenes. A video that is mostly bullets is a slide deck with a voice.

## 4. Vertical (9:16) specifics

- The first frame must already say something: no logo intro, no "hey guys".
- Fewer words on screen (≤ 6 per line, ≤ 4 bullets), bigger numbers.
- Captions on — most people watch muted.
- 20–45 s is the sweet spot for explainers. Loop-friendly endings (the last line sets up the first) help replays.

## 5. Make it yours, not a template

- Pick an angle: a misconception to correct, a surprising comparison, a "how much does it really cost" — not "everything about X".
- One concrete example beats three abstract ones.
- Different topics deserve different themes and pacing; don't ship ten videos that look and sound identical (platforms demote mass-produced, repetitive content).
